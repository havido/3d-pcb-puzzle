The Operation game fits the copper-tape board well, because the sensing needs no electronic components. I've updated [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md) so the goose Operation game is the current goal.

## How it works electrically

- **Wall touch:**
  - The tweezers are wired to the badge's ground.
  - Each copper zone on the goose is wired to one badge button line.
  - When the tweezers touch copper, the badge sees that button as pressed.
- **Seat pads:**
  - Each organ's resting spot has two small copper pads, one to ground and one to a button line.
  - A copper-wrapped organ sitting on them bridges the two.
  - The button reads "held" while the organ is seated and "released" when it is lifted.
- **Button lines:** four lines cover the game.

| Line | Meaning |
|---|---|
| D-pad 1 | Wall touched (penalty) |
| D-pad 2 | Organ A seated in its origin spot (released = picked up) |
| D-pad 3 | Organ B seated in its destination spot (pressed = delivered, win) |
| D-pad 4 | Spare: a second wall zone, so the screen can show where you touched |

- **Player buttons:** A, B and Home stay free for the player to start and restart the game.
- **Line to avoid:** never wire to the BOOT button's line.
- **Missed touches (risk):** a touch may last only a few milliseconds, and the app may not check the buttons often enough to catch it.
  - The fix is one capacitor of about 10 µF from the wall line to ground.
  - A touch discharges it instantly and it refills slowly, so the "press" is held for about 100 ms.
  - Test without the capacitor first.

## Decisions that are yours to make

1. **Where the badge goes.**
   - **Recommended: goose over the buttons.**
     - The goose body overhangs the bottom, button half of the badge. It carries the spring pins pointing down and leaves the display visible.
     - The thin print above the bottom two LEDs lets the goose glow red on a touch.
     - The tactile buttons are 5 mm tall, so it needs pockets for them.
   - **Fallback: clip and ribbon.** A small clip-on spring-pin bridge with a ribbon of wires runs to a goose sitting beside the badge.
2. **Loose organ with tweezers, or a wand with the organ fixed on its tip.**
   - A loose organ is the real Operation game and lets the seat pads work.
   - It needs the organ to be conductive (copper-wrapped) and the channel floor to be plain plastic.
3. **Where the copper goes in the channels.**
   - **Try first: copper on the channel rims.**
     - This means copper on the flat top face along the channel edges, folded slightly over the lip. It fits your flat sandwich-cutter process.
     - The organ then needs a flange, or the tweezers have to ride at rim height.
   - **Alternative: line the vertical walls by hand.**

Which of the two goes on the final goose is one of the two key coupon results.

## Tasks

**Gate 0: checks on a real badge, within the hour**
- Jumper a D-pad pad to ground and confirm an app sees it.
- Repeat through tape and tweezers, and note whether short touches register.
- Find out how badge apps are written. Today's workshop and badge.hackthenorth.com cover this:
  - the language
  - how buttons are read
  - how often they are checked
  - the display and LED calls

**You (physical)**
- **Coupon:**
  - channel widths of 6, 8, 10 and 12 mm, with one curve
  - rim copper compared with wall copper
  - a seat-pad pair
  - the sandwich-cutter clearance steps
- **Organs:** two of them, copper-wrapped, sized from the coupon result.
- **Dock:**
  - registers on the badge outline
  - spring pins on 4 button pads plus the ground test point
  - test it on a scrap print before it touches the badge
- **Tweezers tether:** a wire to board ground with strain relief.
- **Final goose:** tape it, wire it and check continuity.

**SW1: generator**
- Draw the goose in KiCad:
  - the outline is the board edge
  - the channels are cut-outs
  - the copper zones are filled shapes, each with a net name
- The script turns the KiCad file into a top plate and a cutter plate.
- Every dimension is a named parameter.
- This is harder than straight traces because it needs arbitrary outlines and filled zones, so start with extruding the outline and the zone polygons.
- **Fallback:** you model it by hand in CAD.

**SW2: badge game**
- A state machine: idle → remove organ A → deliver organ B → win or lose.
- A countdown with a penalty per touch, and a 0.5 s lockout so one scrape doesn't count ten times.
- LED flashes.

**SW3: feel and pitch**
- **Diagnostic app:** first, an app that shows all 8 button lines live. Everyone will use it for debugging.
- **Animations:** the goose, the touch flinch, win and lose.
- **Pitch:**
  - the line is "a perfboard can't be goose-shaped and an ordered circuit board can't arrive tonight"
  - run a live timer of the build

**Order of work**
1. Gate 0.
2. Print the coupon while the app skeleton and the KiCad goose get started.
3. The dock and a small one-channel playable version.
4. The full goose.
5. Polish.
